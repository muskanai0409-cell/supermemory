Course setter’s note

Tim Denning wrote this at 38 for anyone in their 20s or 30s. Each lesson is a line or two, and blunt. On this map every lesson is a control, a circle you have to reach. Run them in order and you have his argument.

His words are printed in purple, exactly as he wrote them. We drew the 28 lessons we could trace to his published text, so the essay may hold a few more. Where we could not confirm his exact words, the lesson is in black.

Rough ground

Why listen to a 38-year-old

Not much fazes me anymore.

I’ve been part of so much drama –

  • personal bankruptcies
  • homeless friends
  • my battles with drugs and alcohol
  • nearly getting knifed to death
  • friends in jail
  • job losses
  • mental illness
  • $1.2M theft
  • online trolling
  • going from entrepreneur to corporate to entrepreneur, etc.

His list, in his order and his words. He gives no dates. The purple line goes straight through.

Route choice

The difference shows at 35

“When you turn 35 you’ll see the difference between those who took risks and those who didn’t.

How old you feel comes down to how you lived. Not taking risks leads to regrets which ages you faster.”

The course

28 controls, start to finish

Controls 1–3 are his first three lessons, in his order. We grouped the rest by subject. […] marks where we trimmed a quote.

  1. Start

    Wherever you are, 20–39.

  2. 1
    Control 1.

    When you turn 35 you’ll see the difference between those who took risks and those who didn’t.

    Drawn in full above.

  3. 2
    Control 2.

    Having kids isn’t the end of freedom, it’s the beginning. It’ll give you meaning that’ll fuel your greatness.

  4. 3
    Control 3.

    Chasing obsession is the fastest way to become a high performer.

  5. Path & risk · controls 4–6

  6. 4
    Control 4.

    Never follow the conventional path.

    It’s full of boredom and has the most competition.

    He sums up that path as a degree, debt, a job you pretend is your life’s work, and marrying the first person who shows interest.

  7. 5
    Control 5.

    Take your parent’s plan for your life & light it on fire. The best path is one you create.

  8. 6
    Control 6.

    The more hard things you do the more successful you become.

    These challenges forced me to develop mental muscle and acquire skills most didn’t have.

    He means his childhood. He grew up in a rough area, and his family lost both their homes to bankruptcy.

  9. Mind & standards · controls 7–10

  10. 7
    Control 7.

    Raise your standards so high people think you’re a psychopath.

    Screw being average.

    When you raise your standards life gets easier. A lot of decisions are automated for you. […] As long as you stick to your higher than normal standards the rewards that come with them are a given … and they’re huge. More money, time, freedom, energy, happiness, and fulfillment.

  11. 8
    Control 8.

    One of the clearest signs of intelligence is being able to hear an opinion you disagree with and not act emotionally.

    The people you disagree with can teach you the most.

  12. 9
    Control 9.

    Turn off news and politics to naturally increase your IQ.

  13. 10
    Control 10.

    Go visit a retirement home so you know what dying feels like. Then use it as motivation.

  14. Work & business · controls 11–17

  15. 11
    Control 11.

    No one who climbs the corporate ladder is happy. It’s a trap. Choose freedom.

    If you want to be happier then get the hell off the corporate ladder. Stop worrying about promotions, pay rises, and job titles and go build something online that gives you actual freedom.

    He cites his friend Dickie Bush, who left BlackRock after meeting the people 10 and 20 years ahead of him and finding none of them happy.

  16. 12
    Control 12.

    Corporations pretend to care about people to protect their corporate image and lure more victims, but layoff culture shows they don’t care at all. One minute “thoughts and prayers.” Next minute “you’ve been let go.”

  17. 13
    Control 13.

    Society is full of people who need things. Either you will go about the task of seeing to those needs by learning a unique set of skills, or the world will reject you, no matter how inoffensive and courteous you are.

    The line echoes David Wong’s 2012 Cracked essay “6 Harsh Truths That Will Make You a Better Person.”

  18. 14
    Control 14.

    Understand human psychology and you can make as much money as you want.

    Business is just a psychology masterclass.

    Life is too.

    Most people who fail just don’t understand human nature.

  19. 15
    Control 15.

    Writing online is the easiest way to attract more opportunities so you can stop asking for permission.

  20. 16
    Control 16.

    Disappear from public life. Adopt a nickname. Then build something online with zero fear.

  21. 17
    Control 17.

    He ranks flow states above productivity.

    Our wording. We could not confirm his exact sentence.

  22. Money · controls 18–22

  23. 18
    Control 18.

    If you don’t learn how money works you will always work for money.

  24. 19
    Control 19.

    Money isn’t evil. It’s a resource that’ll help you reach personal freedom.

  25. 20
    Control 20.

    Read every popular finance book and invest money early on.

  26. 21
    Control 21.

    Read “The Bitcoin Standard.” Then buy Bitcoin before major governments like the U.S. do.

  27. 22
    Control 22.

    Live in the city while you’re young to network. Then as you get older move to the outer suburbs to save money and reduce debt.

  28. Body · controls 23–26

  29. 23
    Control 23.

    Keeping your dopamine addiction under control will help you more than any other health hack.

    How you invest your dopamine will either uplift you or leave you feeling bored and unmotivated.

  30. 24
    Control 24.

    Video games are fine when you’re younger. But when you’re older they become a time suck.

  31. 25
    Control 25.

    Exercise every day, preferably in nature. It’ll keep you younger for longer.

    Orienteering, the sport this map copies, counts.

  32. 26
    Control 26.

    Give up alcohol. It’s a scam that’ll steal your energy & help you sleep with strangers & get STDs.

  33. People · controls 27–28

  34. 27
    Control 27.

    As you get older you’ll want to leave your loser friends behind. It’s normal.

    Later in life you don’t give a f*ck about looking good, so your friends change. […] Don’t rudely cut them off. Just slowly reduce contact with the ones who you don’t vibe with anymore.

  35. 28
    Control 28.

    Spend more time with your parents than feels normal. Soon they’ll be gone.
